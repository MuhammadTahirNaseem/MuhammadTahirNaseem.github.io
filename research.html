Research

Quantum thermodynamics, open quantum systems, and engineered thermal devices.

My research investigates how heat, information, coherence, and dissipation shape the behavior of quantum systems. The work combines theoretical modeling, master-equation methods, and physically motivated device proposals.

Research Themes

Current Areas of Interest

Quantum Thermodynamics and Heat Transport

Quantum thermodynamics studies energy, work, heat, entropy, and irreversibility in systems where quantum coherence and fluctuations are important. In heat transport problems, the central question is how energy flows through nanoscale or quantum systems coupled to thermal reservoirs. This theme connects microscopic quantum dynamics with macroscopic thermodynamic behavior. It is especially relevant when conventional thermal intuition must be revised at small scales.

Possible Applications

Nanoscale heat management, quantum energy devices, thermal control in quantum technologies, and foundations of thermodynamics.

  • Quantum heat transport
  • Entropy production
  • Non-equilibrium dynamics
  • Thermal reservoirs

Floquet Quantum Thermal Devices

Floquet systems are driven periodically in time, allowing their energy structure and transport properties to be controlled dynamically. In quantum thermal devices, such driving can be used to tune heat currents, enhance performance, or create operating regimes that are not available in static systems. Theoretical models of Floquet devices help clarify how external modulation interacts with dissipation and thermal exchange. This provides a route to designing controllable quantum engines, refrigerators, and heat pumps.

Possible Applications

Driven quantum heat engines, quantum refrigerators, heat pumps, tunable thermal circuits, and coherent control of thermal machines.

  • Floquet engineering
  • Periodic driving
  • Quantum engines
  • Driven dissipation

Quantum Thermal Diodes and Rectification

A thermal diode allows heat to flow more efficiently in one direction than the other, producing thermal rectification. In quantum systems, this directional behavior can arise from asymmetry, nonlinear interactions, structured reservoirs, or quantum coherence. Studying quantum thermal diodes helps identify design principles for controlling heat currents at microscopic scales. These models are useful for building thermal components analogous to electronic circuit elements.

Possible Applications

Quantum thermal circuits, directional heat control, nanoscale thermal logic, thermal protection, and heat-current regulation.

  • Thermal rectification
  • Heat diodes
  • Asymmetric coupling
  • Quantum transport

Quantum Thermometry and Fisher Information

Quantum thermometry asks how temperature can be estimated with high precision using quantum probes. Fisher information provides a rigorous way to quantify the sensitivity of a measurement strategy and the best possible precision allowed by the probe state. This research theme explores how interactions, coherence, squeezing, and environmental structure affect temperature estimation. It links practical sensing protocols with the statistical foundations of quantum metrology.

Possible Applications

Precision temperature sensing, nanoscale thermometry, quantum sensors, cold-atom platforms, and diagnostics of small thermal environments.

  • Quantum metrology
  • Fisher information
  • Temperature estimation
  • Quantum probes

Reservoir-Engineered Mechanical Cat States

Reservoir engineering uses controlled dissipation to prepare and stabilize desired quantum states. Mechanical cat states are superpositions of distinct mechanical configurations and are important examples of macroscopic quantum behavior. By designing the interaction between a mechanical mode and its environment, it may be possible to protect nonclassical states against ordinary decoherence. This theme connects open-system theory with state preparation in optomechanical and hybrid quantum platforms.

Possible Applications

Mechanical quantum information processing, tests of macroscopic quantum coherence, protected nonclassical states, and hybrid quantum devices.

  • Reservoir engineering
  • Cat states
  • Optomechanics
  • Decoherence control

Open Quantum Systems and Master Equations

Open quantum systems provide the framework for studying quantum dynamics in the presence of environmental interactions. Master equations describe how a system evolves when it exchanges energy, particles, or information with its surroundings. This theme includes Markovian and non-Markovian dynamics, dissipative processes, and the validity of common approximations. These methods support much of the modeling used in quantum thermodynamics, quantum optics, and quantum technologies.

Possible Applications

Modeling dissipative quantum devices, quantum optics, solid-state quantum systems, noise analysis, and controlled environment design.

  • Master equations
  • Dissipation
  • Non-Markovian dynamics
  • Quantum noise

Approach

Theoretical Modeling

The research program combines analytical methods and numerical modeling to study energy exchange, information flow, and dissipation in quantum systems. The aim is to develop clear physical mechanisms that can guide future experiments and support the design of quantum thermal technologies.
