Course

ES111: Applied Probability and Statistics

An introductory course in probability, statistics, uncertainty, and data-based reasoning for science and engineering students.

Semester

Spring 2026

The schedule below is designed as a student-friendly guide and can be updated as the semester progresses.

Textbook

Walpole et al.

Probability and Statistics for Engineers and Scientists, 9th edition, by Ronald E. Walpole, Raymond H. Myers, Sharon L. Myers, and Keying Ye.

Overview

Course Overview

ES111 introduces the mathematical foundations and practical use of probability and statistics. Students will learn how to describe random phenomena, work with probability distributions, estimate unknown quantities, test hypotheses, and interpret data with appropriate caution.

The course emphasizes both conceptual understanding and problem-solving technique. Examples are selected to support later work in engineering, physical sciences, laboratory analysis, and quantitative research.

Schedule

Weekly Lecture Topics

Week 1

Introduction to probability, data, samples, populations, and uncertainty.

Week 2

Counting techniques, events, conditional probability, independence, and Bayes' theorem.

Week 3

Random variables, probability mass functions, probability density functions, and cumulative distributions.

Week 4

Expectation, variance, moments, covariance, and correlation.

Week 5

Discrete distributions: binomial, geometric, negative binomial, hypergeometric, and Poisson distributions.

Week 6

Continuous distributions: uniform, exponential, normal, gamma, and related models.

Week 7

Sampling distributions, central limit theorem, and preparation for the midterm examination.

Week 8

Point estimation, confidence intervals, and interpretation of statistical uncertainty.

Week 9

Hypothesis testing for means, variances, and proportions.

Week 10

Two-sample inference, paired data, and comparison of populations.

Week 11

Simple linear regression, correlation, residuals, and model interpretation.

Week 12

Goodness-of-fit tests, contingency tables, and chi-square methods.

Week 13

Review of applications, integrated problem solving, and final examination preparation.

Assessment

Assignments10%

Students should solve assigned problems regularly. Probability and statistics are best learned through steady practice rather than last-minute review.

Textbook

Reading Guide

Primary readings are from Probability and Statistics for Engineers and Scientists, 9th edition, by Walpole et al. Students should read the relevant chapter section before lecture and revisit solved examples after attempting the assigned problems.

  • Before lecture: skim definitions, theorems, and worked examples.
  • After lecture: solve selected exercises without looking at solutions first.
  • Before quizzes: review concepts, formulas, and common mistakes from assignments.
  • Before exams: practice mixed problems that combine probability models, estimation, and inference.
